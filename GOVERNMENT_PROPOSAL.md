# Preliminary Public-Sector Proposal — Solar Agro Tree

## Executive summary

Solar Agro Tree proposes a modular agrivoltaic infrastructure system for selected arid sites. Elevated photovoltaic canopies are arranged on a periodic hexagonal network to create broad dynamic shade while preserving most ground area for agriculture.

The same infrastructure generates electricity for water pumping, precision irrigation, treatment, cold storage, agricultural processing, monitoring, and possible grid export.

The project does **not** assume that all arid land is agriculturally suitable, nor that groundwater is unlimited. Site selection must begin with hydrogeology, water quality, soil suitability, environmental constraints, and agricultural economics.

## Reference 1 km² concept

- land area: 100 ha;
- canopy diameter: 11 m;
- canopy-edge height: 5 m;
- apex height: 8 m;
- hexagonal land-cell side: 12 m;
- theoretical periodic tree count: ~8,019;
- simulated summer instantaneous shading: ~65–66%;
- simulated mean summer shadow exposure: ~9.4 h/m²/day on the reference day;
- preliminary DC capacity: ~147.5 MWp;
- preliminary annual generation envelope: ~199–221 GWh;
- practical agricultural target after roads/services: ~90–94 ha.

## Why this project is different

A conventional solar project primarily optimizes electrical output. This concept is designed to provide simultaneous functions:

1. reduce direct solar load on crops and soil;
2. generate electricity above agricultural ground;
3. operate water pumping, storage, treatment, and drip irrigation;
4. create a more manageable agricultural microclimate;
5. support cold-chain and agricultural processing;
6. scale through a repeatable modular geometry.

## Critical condition: sustainable water

The electrical system can provide significant pumping energy, but energy availability does not prove water availability. Every pilot site must therefore include:

- hydrogeological survey;
- pumping tests;
- water-quality testing;
- sustainable abstraction limit;
- soil salinity and drainage assessment.

## Proposed development path

### Phase 1 — One full-scale solar tree

Validate structure, wind response, foundation, fabrication, PV installation, maintenance, and real cost.

### Phase 2 — Seven hexagons / 31 trees

Validate shadow simulation, microclimate, PV production, irrigation, soil response, and multi-crop trials.

### Phase 3 — Approximately 10 ha demonstration

Validate real agricultural operations, water accounting, maintenance, logistics, and economics.

### Phase 4 — 1 km² deployment

Proceed only after the measured data supports structural, water, agricultural, electrical, and economic feasibility.

## Requested public-sector support at concept stage

The initial request can remain limited to:

- assistance selecting a technically suitable pilot location;
- access to existing soil and water data;
- facilitation of surveys and permits;
- connection with relevant engineering, agricultural, university, and water-resource institutions;
- support for a measurable prototype and pilot program.

## Governance principle

Pilot results should be recorded transparently, including negative results. Scale-up should occur only when measured evidence replaces the current concept assumptions.

## Conclusion

Solar Agro Tree is not simply a proposal to build a solar farm in the desert. It is an attempt to use intense solar exposure as both the energy source and the physical basis for creating agricultural shade, water infrastructure, and productive land use.
