# Water Model

## Principle

Solar energy can make pumping inexpensive in energy terms, but electricity does not create water. The primary water constraint is sustainable water availability, not pump power.

No site should proceed without hydrogeological investigation and a sustainable-yield assessment.

## Pumping-energy equation

The theoretical hydraulic energy required to lift water is:

`E = ρ × g × Q × H / η`

where:

- `ρ` = water density;
- `g` = gravitational acceleration;
- `Q` = water volume;
- `H` = total dynamic head;
- `η` = combined pump/motor/system efficiency.

The reference concept calculation assumes 65% combined efficiency.

Approximate pumped water per electrical kWh:

| Total dynamic head | Water per kWh |
|---|---:|
| 100 m | ~2.39 m³ |
| 200 m | ~1.19 m³ |
| 300 m | ~0.80 m³ |

## Energy-limited theoretical pumping

Using the preliminary central electricity case of ~575 MWh/day:

| Head | Energy-limited theoretical volume |
|---|---:|
| 100 m | ~1.37 million m³/day |
| 200 m | ~686,000 m³/day |
| 300 m | ~457,000 m³/day |

These numbers are **not well yields**. They only show that the electrical system could theoretically supply far more pumping energy than the reference agricultural area requires.

## Irrigation demand framework

One millimetre of irrigation over one hectare equals 10 m³.

Reference scenarios:

| Net irrigation | Water/ha/day |
|---|---:|
| 3 mm/day | 30 m³ |
| 5 mm/day | 50 m³ |
| 7 mm/day | 70 m³ |

For 100 ha at 5 mm/day:

- water requirement: ~5,000 m³/day.

At 300 m total head and 65% combined efficiency, the pumping electricity requirement is approximately 6.3 MWh/day.

Relative to ~575 MWh/day average preliminary generation, this is approximately 1.1% of average project electricity.

The conclusion is not that unlimited water can be pumped. The conclusion is that **water-resource sustainability is likely to dominate energy availability as the limiting factor**.

## Water-source hierarchy

Candidate sources must be assessed site by site:

- sustainable groundwater;
- treated wastewater;
- seasonal runoff harvesting;
- rainwater harvesting from canopies;
- brackish-water treatment where economically justified;
- imported water from nearby infrastructure where available.

## Hydrogeological requirements

Before sizing wells:

- exploratory drilling where required;
- static and dynamic water levels;
- step-drawdown test;
- constant-rate pumping test;
- recovery test;
- aquifer transmissivity and storage estimates;
- sustainable abstraction limit;
- salinity/EC;
- major ions and agricultural water-quality analysis;
- long-term monitoring strategy.

## Irrigation architecture

Preferred baseline:

- sectorized drip irrigation;
- low-pressure distribution;
- storage reservoirs;
- variable-frequency pumps;
- flow meters;
- pressure sensors;
- soil-moisture sensors;
- automated valves;
- fertigation where agronomically appropriate.

## Water storage instead of excessive batteries

Daytime PV can pump water into reservoirs. Irrigation can then draw from stored water later. This converts part of the project's energy-storage requirement into hydraulic storage and may reduce BESS size.
