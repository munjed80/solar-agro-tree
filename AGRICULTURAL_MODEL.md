# Agricultural Model

## Objective

Determine whether dynamic shade, water availability, soil rehabilitation, and controlled irrigation can improve the agricultural productivity of selected arid sites.

## Agricultural hypothesis

The project does not assume that shade alone converts desert land into farmland. Productive agriculture depends on the combined condition of:

- adequate and sustainable water;
- suitable or improvable soil;
- appropriate crop selection;
- controlled irrigation;
- manageable salinity;
- adequate photosynthetically active radiation;
- acceptable microclimate;
- economically viable yields.

## Reference land use

The theoretical column footprint is below 0.3% of 1 km², but roads, reservoirs, substations, wells, service buildings, and operational clearances reduce practical agricultural area.

Reference planning target:

- 90–94 ha practical agricultural use from a 100 ha site.

This is a planning assumption and must be replaced by a detailed site layout.

## Soil baseline

Every candidate site should be sampled for:

- texture;
- pH;
- electrical conductivity;
- sodium/sodicity;
- organic matter;
- calcium carbonate;
- nutrients;
- effective rooting depth;
- infiltration;
- water-holding capacity.

Potential treatments may include compost, organic amendments, gypsum where sodicity requires it, mulching, cover crops, and other site-specific rehabilitation practices.

## Crop-selection principle

The project should optimize value and resilience per unit of water rather than gross yield alone.

Key performance metric:

`economic agricultural value / m³ irrigation water`

Pilot trials should include several categories rather than one crop:

- drought/heat-tolerant field crops;
- forage crops;
- legumes;
- shade-tolerant or shade-benefiting crops;
- selected horticultural crops;
- perennial crops only where water security supports long-term planting.

## Shade experiment

The current D11/S12 model produces approximately 65–66% instantaneous summer shading in the periodic central area and approximately 9.4 cumulative shadow-hours per m² on the summer-solstice reference day.

This is not yet an agronomic optimum.

Field experiments should compare crop response under different effective shade regimes, for example:

- open-sun control;
- approximately 40–50% shade;
- approximately 55–60% shade;
- approximately 65% shade;
- higher shade only where a crop trial justifies it.

## Measurements

Each trial plot should record:

- soil moisture;
- soil temperature;
- air temperature;
- relative humidity;
- PAR/light;
- irrigation volume;
- plant growth;
- phenology;
- yield;
- product quality;
- salinity trend;
- water-use efficiency.

## Irrigation

Baseline irrigation method: drip irrigation with sector-level flow measurement. Flood irrigation is not part of the reference concept.

## Success metrics

The agricultural pilot should be judged using:

- tonnes/ha;
- market value/ha;
- m³ water/tonne;
- market value/m³ water;
- soil-moisture retention;
- crop-temperature reduction;
- survival and quality;
- long-term soil salinity.

## Status

Agricultural performance is **unvalidated** until multi-season field trials are completed.
