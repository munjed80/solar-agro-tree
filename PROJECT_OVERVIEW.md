# Project Overview

## Mission

Solar Agro Tree is a modular agrivoltaic infrastructure concept intended to make selected arid land more suitable for agriculture by combining dynamic shade, elevated solar generation, water pumping, storage, treatment, precision irrigation, and digital monitoring.

The project is designed for regions where land is abundant but extreme solar exposure, heat, evaporation, water scarcity, and weak infrastructure constrain agriculture.

## System logic

The system uses a hollow structural column carrying a six-face pyramidal photovoltaic canopy. The columns repeat on a honeycomb geometry. The photovoltaic surface is elevated above the agricultural ground, so the principal land take comes from foundations, roads, electrical stations, reservoirs, and service areas rather than from the PV modules themselves.

The reference design uses:

- 11 m canopy diameter;
- 5 m canopy-edge height;
- 8 m apex height;
- 12 m land-cell side;
- periodic hexagonal repetition;
- approximately 8,019 trees per km²;
- approximately 65–66% instantaneous summer shading in the central periodic model.

## Why the hexagonal pattern?

The honeycomb pattern provides a repeatable geometry, equal angular distribution, shared corner trees between neighboring cells, and a predictable agricultural spacing. A single land cell appears to contain seven trees: one at its center and six at its vertices. In an infinite periodic network, each vertex tree is shared by three cells. Therefore the effective count is three trees per cell.

## Project outputs

A mature project would produce:

- agricultural crops;
- electricity;
- pumped and stored irrigation water;
- operational data;
- potential grid export;
- potential cold-chain and processing services.

## Reference scale

The repository uses 1 km² as the reference study unit because it is large enough to expose infrastructure-scale questions while remaining easy to normalize per hectare and per square kilometre.

## Engineering principle

The project must not be justified by one metric alone. Its performance should be evaluated through a combined scorecard:

- shade-hours per m²;
- crop yield per hectare;
- crop value per m³ of water;
- electricity generation;
- pumping energy;
- water sustainability;
- structural cost per tree;
- maintenance cost;
- total economic output per hectare.

## Current status

The project is currently at concept-engineering / pre-feasibility level. Geometry and shadow behavior have been modelled. Energy and pumping figures are preliminary. Structural loads, hydrogeology, crop response, manufacturing cost, geotechnical design, and grid interconnection remain open engineering workstreams.

## Development philosophy

The system should be validated progressively rather than scaled immediately:

1. Full-scale single-tree prototype.
2. Seven-hexagon / 31-tree pilot.
3. Agricultural demonstration at approximately 10 ha.
4. 1 km² deployment only after measured validation.
