# Risks and Limitations

## 1. Water-resource risk — critical

The project can generate pumping energy, but it cannot guarantee sustainable water supply. Over-abstraction could damage aquifers and invalidate the agricultural model.

Mitigation: hydrogeological survey, pumping tests, sustainable-yield limits, water-quality testing, continuous groundwater monitoring, and diversified water sources.

## 2. Structural wind risk — critical

An 11 m elevated canopy presents significant wind area and overturning load.

Mitigation: site-specific wind study, structural modelling, fatigue design, aerodynamic review, full-scale prototype, independently reviewed foundation design.

## 3. Agricultural shade risk — high

Approximately 65% dynamic shade may benefit some crops and reduce yield for others.

Mitigation: replicated crop trials, open-sun controls, PAR measurements, multiple shading densities before scale-up.

## 4. Cost risk — high

The elevated structure is more complex than conventional ground-mounted PV. Steel, foundations, installation labour, corrosion protection, maintenance, and custom module geometry may dominate CAPEX.

Mitigation: design-to-cost process, standard modules where possible, structural optimization, local fabrication study, prototype bill of materials.

## 5. Soiling and cleaning — high

Arid environments can produce substantial dust accumulation.

Mitigation: cleaning-access design, soiling sensors, low-water cleaning methods, optimized tilt and drainage, automated/semi-automated cleaning assessment.

## 6. PV orientation and mismatch — medium/high

The six faces have different azimuths and production profiles.

Mitigation: face-level yield simulation, appropriate MPPT grouping, electrical monitoring, optimizer comparison where justified.

## 7. Grid interconnection — high at 1 km² scale

A preliminary capacity near 147.5 MWp is utility-scale and may exceed local network hosting capacity.

Mitigation: early grid study, phased connection, local agricultural loads, possible curtailment modelling, storage/offtake strategy.

## 8. Ground conditions — high

Foundation type and cost depend on geotechnical conditions.

Mitigation: boreholes, soil tests, bearing/uplift analysis, corrosion/aggressivity testing.

## 9. Water salinity and soil salinization — critical agricultural risk

Poor-quality groundwater can damage crops and soils even when water quantity is adequate.

Mitigation: full water chemistry, drainage strategy, salt balance, crop selection, treatment where viable.

## 10. Operational complexity — medium/high

Thousands of elevated structures, electrical strings, sensors, valves, and irrigation sectors create a large maintenance system.

Mitigation: standardized components, asset IDs, digital maintenance records, sector isolation, spare-parts strategy, remote monitoring.

## 11. Security and vandalism — site dependent

Remote infrastructure may face theft or damage.

Mitigation: site security assessment, protected electrical equipment, tamper-resistant access, monitoring, local operational presence.

## 12. Environmental and permitting risk

Land conversion, groundwater abstraction, transmission lines, and civil works can require environmental and regulatory approvals.

Mitigation: environmental screening at site-selection stage and early engagement with competent authorities.

## Model limitations

Current models do not constitute:

- structural certification;
- hydrogeological proof;
- crop-yield prediction;
- bankable PV yield assessment;
- detailed financial feasibility;
- environmental impact assessment;
- issued-for-construction drawings.

The repository should be updated continuously as measured data replaces assumptions.
